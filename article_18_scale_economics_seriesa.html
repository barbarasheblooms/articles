How to prove unit economics scale, not just work today

By SheBlooms 7 min read

At Series A, healthy unit economics today are assumed. The question is whether margin, retention, and pricing hold, or improve, as the company gets much bigger.

The business model slide at Series A needs to answer a harder question than at Seed: not just "does this work", but "does this keep working, or get better, once you are five times the size". That requires showing margin, retention economics, and pricing evolution with a scale lens, not just a snapshot.

Show gross margin against the benchmark, not in isolation

A gross margin number means little without context. State it against the reference range for your business type (60 to 70%+ is the common SaaS expectation) and be ready to explain any gap, whether it is closing, and what specifically closes it.

1
Gross margin, benchmarked
State the number, the reference range for comparable businesses, and the trend, improving, flat, or declining, over the last several quarters.
2
Net revenue retention
This single metric tells investors whether your existing customer base is growing or shrinking in revenue terms even before you add a single new customer. Above 100% is the commonly cited target.
3
Pricing evolution with growth
Explain how pricing changes as you move upmarket or add tiers, and whether that evolution improves or dilutes your average unit economics.
Doesn't prove scale
  • Gross margin stated with no benchmark or trend
  • No mention of net revenue retention
  • Pricing shown as static, with no evolution story
Proves scale
  • Gross margin benchmarked against peers, trend shown
  • Net revenue retention stated, ideally above 100%
  • Pricing evolution explained and tied to unit economics
A scale-proof economics statement
"Gross margin: 68%, in line with SaaS benchmarks, up from 61% two quarters ago as we automated onboarding. Net revenue retention: 114%. Enterprise tier pricing, introduced this year, carries 30% higher gross margin than our original plan."

"The question shifted from 'does the model work' to 'does it get better as you grow'. Once we could show margin improving with scale instead of staying flat, that answered it."

SheBlooms founder
What Series A investors check

Gross margin benchmarked against your business type, with a trend shown. Net revenue retention stated clearly. Pricing evolution explained, and tied to whether unit economics improve with growth or dilute.

Key takeaways
  • State gross margin against a real benchmark, and show the trend, not just a snapshot.
  • Net revenue retention shows whether the existing base grows before a single new customer is added.
  • Explain how pricing evolves with growth, and whether that helps or hurts unit economics.
  • Prove economics scale by showing multiple quarters of trend, not a single data point.
  • Be ready to discuss ESOP design and senior team compensation as part of this conversation.
