How to test pricing with real customers before you have a model

By SheBlooms 6 min read

At Pre-Seed nobody expects a proven business model. They expect proof that a real person has actually paid the price you are proposing.

A theoretical pricing table is easy to build and tells an investor almost nothing. What they are actually checking for is whether anyone outside your own head has agreed to pay, even a small number of people, at even a modest price. That single data point outweighs an elaborate pricing strategy with zero revenue behind it.

Charge something before you have the full product

You do not need a finished product to test pricing. A manual, concierge, or waitlist-with-deposit version can validate willingness to pay well before the product is fully built. The goal is a real transaction, not a survey answer about what someone might pay hypothetically.

1
Pick one price and ask for it directly
Resist the urge to test five pricing tiers at once. Pick your best guess, quote it plainly, and see what happens. You will learn more from ten real conversations at one price than from a survey with fifty hypothetical answers.
2
Track first revenue, however small
Even a handful of customers paying a small amount is a different kind of evidence than zero. Record it precisely: who paid, how much, and for what version of the product.
3
Estimate CAC and LTV roughly, but honestly
You will not have clean data yet. A rough estimate, clearly labeled as an estimate, is far more credible than pretending you have precise numbers this early.
A credible early pricing statement
"We charge €35/month. 12 founders have paid it so far, with zero discounting. Rough CAC is €90 from organic content; LTV is still early to call, but average tenure so far is 3.5 months."

State your use of funds and runway plainly

At this stage, the financial conversation embedded in the business model slide is mostly about how you will use the money and for how long it lasts. Be specific: what hires, what channels, what milestones, and the runway that results from the raise you are asking for.

"The moment we stopped saying 'freemium with a premium tier planned' and started saying '12 people already pay €35/month', the whole conversation changed."

SheBlooms founder
What this slide needs at Pre-Seed

A real price, tested with real customers, even a small number. First revenue stated precisely. A rough, honestly labeled CAC and LTV estimate. A clear statement of use of funds and expected runway.

Key takeaways
  • A theoretical pricing table with no real transactions behind it will not hold up.
  • Charge something before the product is finished, using a manual or concierge version if needed.
  • Track first revenue precisely: who paid, how much, for what.
  • Rough, honestly labeled CAC and LTV estimates beat false precision.
  • State use of funds and runway plainly and specifically.
