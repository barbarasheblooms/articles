How to calculate and present growth, retention and churn at Seed

By SheBlooms 7 min read

At Seed, this is often the single slide where investors make their decision. Month-over-month growth, retention and churn need to be calculated correctly, and shown honestly.

The traction slide stops being about proving anyone will pay, that was answered at Pre-Seed, and starts being the central evidence for whether this is a fundable company. Investors will recalculate your numbers themselves, so the underlying data needs to hold up under scrutiny.

Calculate month-over-month growth correctly

MoM growth is the percentage change in a metric (customers, revenue) from one month to the next, averaged over a period rather than cherry-picked from your single best month. Show several consecutive months, not just the strongest one.

1
Show several months, not one
A single strong month tells investors little. Several consecutive months, even with some variance, show a real trend rather than a spike.
2
State retention and churn together
Retention (customers who stay) and churn (customers who leave) are two views of the same data. Report both, at a consistent time window (e.g. 60-day or 90-day), so investors can compare against benchmarks.
3
Add engagement, not just headline numbers
Frequency of use, depth of usage, or recurrence data shows whether growth is being driven by genuinely engaged customers or by acquisition alone.
Doesn't hold up to scrutiny
  • MoM growth calculated from a single best month
  • Retention reported with no matching churn number
  • No engagement or recurrence data included
Holds up to scrutiny
  • Several consecutive months of MoM growth shown
  • Retention and churn reported at the same time window
  • Engagement data included alongside the headline numbers
A traction statement that holds up
"18% average MoM growth over the last 4 months (14%, 22%, 16%, 20%). 71% 60-day retention, 29% 60-day churn. Average of 3.2 sessions per week among active customers."

"An investor recalculated our growth number live on the call from the raw data we shared. It matched exactly, and that single moment did more for our credibility than the pitch itself."

What Seed investors check

Several consecutive months of MoM growth, not a cherry-picked one. Retention and churn reported together at a consistent time window. Engagement or recurrence data alongside the headline numbers. Rough CAC and LTV estimates included.

Key takeaways
  • Show several consecutive months of MoM growth, not just your best month.
  • Report retention and churn together, at a consistent time window.
  • Add engagement or recurrence data to show growth is not acquisition alone.
  • Expect investors to recalculate your numbers, so the underlying data needs to hold up.
