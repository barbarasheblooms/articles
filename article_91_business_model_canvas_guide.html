How to fill every block of the Business Model Canvas, with examples

The canvas only works if each block is filled with something specific and testable. Here is what belongs in each of the nine blocks, and what founders write by mistake instead.

Use SheBlooms' fillable Business Model Canvas tool alongside this guide. Fill the blocks in the order below, since each one depends on the block before it. Filling them in a random order is the most common reason a canvas ends up inconsistent.

1Customer Segments

The specific groups of people or organizations you serve, not "everyone who could benefit." If you serve more than one segment, name each one and note which is primary.

Good"Working mothers running a service-based business with 1 to 5 employees, in their first three years of operation."
Weak"Small business owners." Too broad to design a product, a price, or a channel around.

2Value Proposition

The specific problem you solve for that segment and why your way of solving it is better than what they do today, including doing nothing. State it in terms of the outcome the customer gets, not the features you built.

Good"Cuts the time to prepare monthly financial reports from six hours to twenty minutes, with no accounting background required."

3Channels

How the value proposition actually reaches the customer segment: awareness, evaluation, purchase, delivery, and after-sale support. Most early-stage canvases list one channel per stage. List the ones you have actually tested, not the ones you plan to test.

4Customer Relationships

How you acquire, keep, and grow each segment. This is about the type of relationship, not the channel: self-service, personal assistance, community, or automated. A B2B product sold with a high-touch sales process needs a very different cost structure than a self-service product, and this block is where that difference should first show up.

5Revenue Streams

How each segment actually pays: the mechanism (subscription, transaction fee, license, one-time sale), the price, and the frequency. If you have more than one revenue stream, note what share of total revenue each one represents today, not at some future state.

This is the block investors read most carefully, and the one founders most often leave vague. "We will monetize through subscriptions and partnerships" is not a revenue stream, it is a list of options. Pick the primary one and be specific about the number.

6Key Resources

The assets that make the model work and would be hardest to replace: a proprietary dataset, a specific team capability, a piece of technology, a license, or a brand. If you removed this resource, the model would not function. Physical inventory or a generic tech stack usually does not belong here.

7Key Activities

The handful of things your company must do well, every week, for the model to work: building product, running a sales motion, moderating a marketplace, sourcing supply. Three to five activities is normal. A long list usually means the business is not yet focused.

8Key Partnerships

Who you rely on outside your own team: suppliers, distribution partners, technology providers, or strategic alliances. Note what each partnership gives you that you could not build or buy on your own, and what happens to the model if that partner disappears.

9Cost Structure

The costs required to run the activities and resources above, split roughly between fixed costs (rent, salaries, tooling) and variable costs (that scale with each new customer, like payment processing or support). This block should visibly connect to the Revenue Streams block: if your biggest cost scales in step with revenue, you have a different kind of business than one with mostly fixed costs.

10How the blocks check each other

Cross-checks worth running once the canvas is full

  • Does every Customer Segment have a Value Proposition written specifically for it, not a generic one?
  • Does every Revenue Stream trace back to a segment that is willing and able to pay at that price?
  • Do the Channels you listed match the Customer Relationship you described, or are you claiming a self-service model while relying on manual sales calls?
  • Does the Cost Structure include the Key Activities and Key Resources you named, or are there costs on one side with nothing to explain them on the other?

A canvas that passes these checks is not a guarantee of success, but it is a sign the model has been thought through as a system rather than assembled as nine separate answers.
