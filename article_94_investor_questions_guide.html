What Investors Ask at Pre-Seed

By SheBlooms 4 min read

At pre-seed, investors are betting almost entirely on people and thesis. There is no meaningful traction, often no product. What they want to understand is: can this team execute this idea?

The conversation moves through five areas, roughly in order of how much weight they carry. Founding team and problem validation are close to non-negotiable. Product, market, and financials matter, but investors already know each is still incomplete at this stage, and they read them that way.

Founding Team High weight

This is the single most important area at pre-seed. Investors are buying the founders, not the product.

  • Who are the founders, and what is their relevant experience?
  • Do they have prior experience in entrepreneurship or in this specific industry?
  • How are roles divided within the team?
  • Have they worked together before?
  • Do they have key advisors or mentors?

Problem & Solution High weight

The problem needs to be real, specific, and urgent. At this stage, interviews, surveys, and waitlists count as validation.

  • What problem are you solving?
  • Why is this problem relevant and urgent right now?
  • How is the proposed solution better than current alternatives?
  • Have you validated the need with real customers? (Interviews, surveys, and waitlists count here, even without a product)

Product Medium weight

Investors know the product is early. They want to see a credible plan, not a finished platform.

  • What stage is the product in? (Idea, prototype, MVP?)
  • What is the development roadmap?
  • What is the intended competitive advantage? (Technology, business model, market access)

Market & Opportunity Medium weight

They want to see that the market is big enough to matter and that the timing makes sense.

  • What is the TAM, SAM, and SOM?
  • Who are the main competitors?
  • Is there a market trend that makes this timing favorable?

Financials & Use of Funds Medium weight

At pre-seed, the financial conversation is about how you will use the money, not about complex projections.

  • How much capital are you seeking?
  • How do you plan to use the funds? (Hires, product, marketing)
  • What is the expected runway with this raise?
  • Have you raised capital before? From whom?
What investors typically do NOT ask at pre-seed

Detailed unit economics (CAC, LTV, churn) are not expected since there is not enough data yet. Sophisticated financial projections are largely fictional at this stage and investors know it. Exit strategy is premature, except in conversations with very specific angel investors.

Key takeaways
  • Founding team and problem validation carry the most weight; there's no way around either.
  • Interviews, surveys, and waitlists are legitimate validation, even with no product built yet.
  • Product, market, and use-of-funds questions test for a credible plan, not a finished answer.
  • Detailed unit economics, polished projections, and exit strategy are not expected yet.
