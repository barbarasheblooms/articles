How to turn GTM into a documented, team-run playbook

By SheBlooms 7 min read

At Series A, growth needs to look like a machine: a documented playbook a team can run, not a set of channels only the founders understand.

By Series A, GTM efficiency underpins the traction numbers investors scrutinize most heavily. A founder-dependent growth motion, however effective, raises a specific concern: what happens when the company needs to scale beyond what founders can personally drive.

Document the playbook, not just the results

Investors want evidence that your GTM motion is written down, teachable, and already being run partly or fully by people who are not the founders. This is what makes aggressive scaling of the sales or marketing team a credible plan rather than a hope.

1
State your Magic Number
This efficiency metric (net new ARR generated per dollar of sales and marketing spend) is a standard reference investors will calculate themselves if you do not provide it.
2
Show the playbook is repeatable across a team
If a new hire followed the documented process, would they get comparable results to the founders? Evidence of this, even partial, is a strong signal.
3
Include strategic partnerships
Distribution partnerships that extend reach beyond your own channels show a more sophisticated, less founder-dependent growth strategy.
Founder-led, not a system
  • Growth entirely dependent on founders personally closing deals
  • No Magic Number or sales efficiency metric stated
  • No documented playbook a new hire could follow
A repeatable machine
  • Magic Number and sales efficiency stated clearly
  • A documented playbook already being run by non-founders
  • Strategic partnerships supporting distribution at scale
A GTM statement that reads as a machine
"Magic Number: 0.9. Our two most recent hires, following the documented onboarding playbook, closed within 15% of founder-led conversion rates in their first quarter. Two distribution partnerships now account for 20% of new signups."
What Series A investors check

Magic Number and overall sales efficiency stated clearly. A documented playbook, repeatable across a team, not dependent on the founders. Strategic partnerships supporting distribution. Payback period and CAC shown at current scale.

Key takeaways
  • State your Magic Number; investors will calculate it if you do not.
  • Show your GTM playbook is documented and repeatable across a team.
  • Evidence from non-founder hires running the playbook is a strong signal.
  • Include strategic partnerships that extend distribution beyond your own channels.
  • GTM efficiency underpins the traction numbers investors scrutinize most at Series A.
