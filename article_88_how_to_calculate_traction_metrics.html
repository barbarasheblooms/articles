How to calculate the traction metrics investors actually check — with formulas and worked examples

SheBloomsTraction

"Growing fast" means nothing without the calculation behind it. Here's exactly how to compute the six numbers that show up in almost every traction conversation, with a worked example for each.

MRR and ARR

Formula
MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue) = sum of all active customers' monthly subscription value. ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) = MRR × 12.
Worked example
40 customers on a €50/month plan and 5 on a €200/month plan: MRR = (40 × €50) + (5 × €200) = €2,000 + €1,000 = €3,000. ARR = €3,000 × 12 = €36,000. One-time or usage-based revenue is not MRR — only count revenue that recurs predictably.

Month-over-month growth rate

Formula
MoM growth % = ((Current month MRR − Previous month MRR) ÷ Previous month MRR) × 100
Worked example
MRR grew from €3,000 to €3,450 in one month: (€3,450 − €3,000) ÷ €3,000 × 100 = 15% MoM growth. Report growth over 3+ consecutive months, not a single lucky month — investors specifically look for the trend, not one data point.

GMV (Gross Merchandise Value)

Formula
GMV = Total value of all transactions processed through your platform in a period (before your take-rate or fees are deducted).
Worked example
A marketplace processes 300 transactions averaging €80 each in a month: GMV = 300 × €80 = €24,000. If you take a 15% commission, your actual revenue is €3,600 — always report both GMV and your take-rate together; GMV alone overstates your actual business.

Retention and churn

Formula
Monthly churn % = (Customers lost during the month ÷ Customers at start of month) × 100. Retention % = 100 − churn %.
Worked example
You start the month with 200 customers and 8 cancel: churn = 8 ÷ 200 × 100 = 4% monthly churn, or 96% monthly retention. A 4% monthly churn compounds to roughly a 60% annual retention rate — always convert monthly figures to an annual view before comparing to annual benchmarks.

Net Revenue Retention (NRR)

Formula
NRR % = ((Starting MRR + Expansion MRR − Contraction MRR − Churned MRR) ÷ Starting MRR) × 100
Worked example
Starting MRR €10,000, existing customers upgrade for +€800 (expansion), some downgrade for −€300 (contraction), and €500 churns out: NRR = (€10,000 + €800 − €300 − €500) ÷ €10,000 × 100 = 100%. Above 100% means existing customers alone are growing your revenue even before counting new customers — a strong signal at Series A.

Repeat usage / repeat purchase rate

Formula
Repeat rate % = (Customers with 2+ purchases or sessions in a period ÷ Total customers in that period) × 100
Worked example
120 customers purchased in a quarter, and 45 of them purchased more than once: 45 ÷ 120 × 100 = 37.5% repeat rate. For non-subscription businesses (e-commerce, marketplaces, usage-based apps), this is often a more relevant health signal than churn, which assumes an ongoing subscription that doesn't exist.

Always show the formula, not just the result

Stating "96% retention" without showing the underlying customer counts invites the question "retention of what, over what period, measured how?" Show the inputs alongside the number — it demonstrates you calculated it correctly and builds trust in every other number on the slide.

Benchmarks vary meaningfully by business model, price point, and industry — a usage-based marketplace and an enterprise SaaS tool should not be judged against the same churn benchmark. Use these as a general reference, not a hard bar.
