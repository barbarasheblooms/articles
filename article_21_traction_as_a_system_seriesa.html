How to prove your traction is a system, not a lucky spike

By SheBlooms 7 min read

At Series A, the traction slide needs to prove consistency built from repeatable channels, backed by cohort-level data, not a single strong quarter.

By Series A, everything else in the deck supports this one slide. Investors are looking for evidence that growth is a system that will keep producing results, rather than a spike created by a single campaign, press mention, or unsustainable discount.

Show three or more months of consistent growth

Consistency across several consecutive months from repeatable channels is worth more than a single dramatic spike. State your MoM growth against the common SaaS reference range of 15 to 20%+, and be honest about which channels produced it.

1
Net revenue retention above 100%
This shows your existing customer base is growing in value even before a single new customer is added, a strong signal of a durable, expanding business rather than one dependent purely on new acquisition.
2
Churn below the common benchmark
State monthly churn against the common SaaS reference of below 5%, and show the trend, is it improving as the product matures, or stuck.
3
Cohort-level CAC, LTV and payback
Show these numbers by cohort, not as a single blended figure, so investors can see whether unit economics are improving as your product and channels mature.
Reads as a spike
  • One strong month attributed to a single campaign or press hit
  • No net revenue retention or churn benchmark stated
  • Blended CAC and LTV with no cohort-level detail
Reads as a system
  • 3+ months of consistent MoM growth from repeatable channels
  • Net revenue retention above 100%, monthly churn below 5%
  • Cohort-level CAC, LTV and payback data, showing the trend
A traction statement that reads as a system
"18%, 19%, 17% and 20% MoM growth over the last 4 months, driven by content and referral, not a single campaign. Net revenue retention: 112%. Monthly churn: 3.8%, improving from 5.1% two quarters ago."

"We stopped showing the one great month from the product launch and started showing the four boring, consistent months after it. That's what convinced them growth was systemic."

SheBlooms founder
What Series A investors check

3+ months of consistent MoM growth from repeatable channels. Net revenue retention above 100%. Monthly churn below 5%, with an improving trend. CAC, LTV and payback backed by real, cohort-level data, not blended estimates.

Key takeaways
  • Show 3+ consecutive months of growth from repeatable channels, not a single spike.
  • State net revenue retention, ideally above 100%.
  • Report monthly churn against the sub-5% SaaS benchmark, with a trend.
  • Use cohort-level CAC, LTV and payback data, not a blended snapshot.
  • This is the center of the Series A conversation; everything else supports it.
