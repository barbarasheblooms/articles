How to build a repeatable 2 to 3 channel mix

By SheBlooms 6 min read

At Seed, a channel without unit economics does not count as traction. Investors expect real CAC and volume data across 2 to 3 core channels, and a plan to scale what already works.

One validated channel was enough at Pre-Seed. By Seed, investors want to see that growth is not dependent on a single, possibly fragile, channel, and that you are actively deepening what already works rather than continuing to search for new options.

State CAC and volume per channel, not blended

Just like unit economics, GTM data hides weaknesses when blended. Break down your 2 to 3 core channels individually, so investors can see which is most efficient and where the real repeatability lives.

1
Show the mix explicitly
Name each core channel, its share of total acquisition, and its individual CAC. This tells investors whether you are diversified or still overly dependent on one source.
2
State the payback period per channel
A channel with a great CAC but a long payback period behaves very differently from one with a modest CAC and fast payback. Both numbers matter.
3
Show you are scaling, not still searching
The plan should focus on deepening the channels that work (more budget, more content, more partnerships) rather than continuing to test new, unproven ones.
Still searching
  • Blended CAC across every channel tried
  • No stated plan beyond "we will keep testing"
  • Overly dependent on a single fragile channel
Scaling what works
  • 2-3 channels with individual CAC and volume stated
  • Payback period stated per channel
  • A specific plan to deepen proven channels
A credible Seed channel mix
"Content: 55% of new customers, CAC €40, payback 1.2 months. Referral: 30%, CAC €25, payback 0.8 months. Paid: 15%, CAC €180, payback 5.1 months, being scaled cautiously."

"Breaking our growth down by channel showed us that referral, which we barely tracked, was actually our most efficient one. We doubled down on it immediately."

What Seed investors check

2 to 3 core channels with individual CAC and volume data. Payback period stated per channel. A plan to scale proven channels rather than keep testing new ones.

Key takeaways
  • A channel without unit economics does not count as traction at Seed.
  • Break down CAC and volume per channel, not blended.
  • State payback period per channel; a low CAC with slow payback still matters.
  • Show you are deepening proven channels, not still searching for new ones.
  • Diversification across 2 to 3 channels reduces dependency risk.
