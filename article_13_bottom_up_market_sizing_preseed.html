How to size your market from the bottom up

By SheBlooms 7 min read

Investors do not expect a perfect number at Pre-Seed. They expect a methodology they can trust, and bottom-up beats top-down every time.

Most first-time founders open a market-sizing slide with a Gartner or Statista figure: a $50B industry number pulled from a report, with no visible connection to the actual business being built. Investors have seen this slide hundreds of times, and it tells them almost nothing except that the founder has not yet done the harder, more useful calculation.

Start from your ICP, not from an industry report

Bottom-up sizing starts with a number you can actually defend: how many people or companies match your Ideal Customer Profile, multiplied by what they would realistically pay you. Everything else, SAM and TAM, is built by zooming out from that number, not the other way around.

1
Count your ICP precisely
Define the ICP narrowly enough that you could plausibly name real companies or people who fit it, then find or estimate how many of them exist in your initial market.
2
Multiply by a realistic price
Use your actual or intended price point, not an aspirational one. This gives you SOM: the number you can realistically capture in the next 2 to 3 years.
3
Zoom out to SAM, then TAM
SAM widens the ICP definition to the full addressable segment within reach (e.g. all of Southern Europe rather than one country); TAM widens further to the full category globally. Both should still trace back to the same per-customer economics.
A defensible bottom-up chain
"There are approximately 40,000 female founders actively fundraising in Southern Europe (SOM population). At €1,200/year, that is a €48M SOM. Widening to all of Europe, SAM is roughly 180,000 founders, or €216M. TAM, all female founders globally who will fundraise, is in the low billions."

Name your competitors and the timing argument in the same breath

A market-sizing slide that never mentions competitors reads as naive. Name the two or three most relevant direct or indirect alternatives, briefly, and pair the market number with a one-line argument for why now: a regulatory shift, a behavior change, a cost curve, anything that explains why this market is opening up at this moment rather than five years ago or five years from now.

Reads as unresearched
  • A single large TAM number with no calculation shown
  • Sourced entirely from a third-party industry report
  • No competitors named, no timing argument
Reads as credible
  • SOM built from your own ICP count and price
  • SAM and TAM shown as zoom-outs from that same number
  • Competitors named and a one-line timing argument included

"The first time we showed a bottom-up number instead of a market report slide, an investor stopped us mid-pitch to say it was the first market slide that week he actually believed."

The chain investors want to see

ICP defined specifically enough to count. SOM calculated as ICP count times realistic price. SAM and TAM as zoom-outs from that same number, not separate research citations. Competitors named. One clear sentence on market timing.

Key takeaways
  • Start from your ICP count and real price, not an industry report figure.
  • SOM comes first: it is the number you can defend in a room.
  • SAM and TAM should zoom out from the same calculation, not cite unrelated sources.
  • Name your competitors briefly, even at Pre-Seed.
  • Pair the market number with a one-line argument for why the timing favors you now.
