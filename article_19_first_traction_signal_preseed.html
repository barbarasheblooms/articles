How to present traction when the numbers are still small

By SheBlooms 6 min read

At Pre-Seed, investors are not expecting scale. They are checking for a real signal that someone besides you believes this is worth paying for.

A handful of paying customers with a positive trend is worth more than a large but unconverted waitlist. The instinct to inflate early numbers, or to hide behind vanity metrics like signups and pageviews, usually backfires: experienced investors know exactly what those numbers are hiding.

Lead with paying customers, however few

State the number plainly, along with what they are paying and for how long they have stuck around. A trend line with three data points moving up is more convincing than a single large but static number.

1
State the real number, not the potential one
"12 paying customers" beats "2,000 signups" every time, because it answers the question investors are actually asking: will someone pay for this.
2
Show the trend, not just the snapshot
Even three months of data showing a small number growing tells a better story than a single larger number with no history behind it.
3
Add qualitative proof
A direct quote from an early customer, a testimonial, or a small case study adds credibility that a raw number alone cannot.
Reads as inflated
  • Leading with signups or waitlist size instead of paying customers
  • A single large number with no trend or history shown
  • No qualitative evidence, quotes, or case studies
Reads as honest and credible
  • Paying customer count and MRR stated plainly, however small
  • A trend shown over at least a few months
  • A direct quote or short case study from an early customer
A credible early traction statement
"12 paying customers, €420 MRR, up from 4 customers three months ago. One customer told us: 'this is the first tool that actually understood my stage instead of treating me like every other founder.'"

"We used to lead with our waitlist number. The moment we switched to our much smaller paying customer number, investors leaned in instead of skimming past."

What this slide needs at Pre-Seed

Paying customers and MRR stated precisely, however small. A trend shown across at least a few months. Qualitative proof: a quote, testimonial, or short case study, if you have one.

Key takeaways
  • Lead with real paying customers, not signups or waitlist size.
  • A small number trending up beats a large static number with no history.
  • Add qualitative proof: a direct quote or short case study.
  • State MRR precisely, even if it is modest.
  • Consistency matters more than absolute size at this stage.
