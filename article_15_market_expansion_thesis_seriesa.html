How to frame your market as an expansion thesis at Series A

By SheBlooms 7 min read

At Series A, the market slide is no longer about proving the opportunity exists. It is about proving you can credibly capture a large share of it as you expand.

By Series A, nobody is questioning whether your initial market is real, your traction already answers that. The market slide's job changes: it needs to show the path from where you are today to the large number the round is being sized against, and address directly whether this is a market where one company can win big, or a fragmented space with no clear winner.

Show the expansion path explicitly

Investors want to see the specific vectors of expansion: new geographies, adjacent customer segments, or cross-sell into your existing base. Each vector should connect to evidence you already have, not just be asserted as a future possibility.

1
New geographies
If your playbook worked in one region, show the specific evidence that it transfers: comparable customer behavior, comparable acquisition cost, or an early pilot in the new geography.
2
Adjacent segments
If you are expanding beyond your original ICP, show the signal that demand already exists there, inbound requests, pilot usage, or a clearly analogous pain point.
3
Cross-sell into your existing base
If part of the expansion thesis is selling more into current customers, your net revenue retention and expansion data should already be showing early signs of this working.

Address the winner-takes-most question directly

Series A investors are sizing the round against how big this can become, which means they need a view on market structure: is this a market where the leader captures a large share (network effects, high switching costs, brand), or is it structurally fragmented, in which case the size number matters less than your specific defensible position within it?

Unaddressed structure
  • Large TAM cited with no view on market concentration
  • Expansion vectors asserted with no supporting evidence
  • No mention of competitors at scale
Addressed structure
  • Explicit view on winner-takes-most vs. fragmented dynamics
  • Each expansion vector backed by early evidence, not just narrative
  • Competitors and differentiation stated at scale, not just today
An expansion thesis with evidence
"Our France pilot, launched with the same playbook as Southern Europe, hit 60% of the same 90-day activation rate in the first cohort. Net revenue retention of 118% shows the cross-sell motion is already working inside existing accounts."

"We stopped citing the global TAM and started showing the France pilot data instead. That's what made the expansion thesis land as evidence rather than ambition."

What holds up at Series A

A specific expansion path (geography, segment, or cross-sell) backed by early evidence for each vector. A direct answer on whether this market rewards a single winner or stays fragmented. Competitors and differentiation named at the scale you are now operating at.

Key takeaways
  • The market slide's job at Series A is proving a credible path to a large share, not proving the market exists.
  • Back each expansion vector, geography, segment, or cross-sell, with early evidence, not just narrative.
  • Address directly whether the market rewards a single winner or is structurally fragmented.
  • Use your own expansion and retention data as proof the cross-sell vector already works.
  • Name competitors and differentiation at the scale you now operate at, not just at launch.
