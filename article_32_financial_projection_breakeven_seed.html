How to build a 12-24 month projection and path to break-even

By SheBlooms 7 min read

The financial conversation gets more detailed at Seed. Investors want to understand your burn, your current MRR, and a specific, assumption-backed path to break-even.

By Seed, you have real revenue and real burn data, and the model needs to reflect that reality rather than restate the Pre-Seed assumptions unchanged. Every number in the projection should have a clear, statable assumption behind it that an investor could question and you could defend.

Build the 12-24 month P&L from real current numbers

Start from your actual current MRR and burn, then project forward using growth rates and cost assumptions you can defend with your own traction data, not industry averages pulled from a benchmark report.

1
State current MRR and burn precisely
These are the anchor points for the entire projection. Get them exactly right before building anything forward from them.
2
Project using your own growth rate, not an industry average
Your MoM growth from the Traction slide should directly inform the revenue projection here. If the two numbers do not match, investors will notice.
3
Show the specific break-even milestone
Name the month and the MRR or customer count at which the business becomes cash-flow positive, tied to a specific, named assumption.
Generic projection
  • Growth rate borrowed from an industry benchmark, not your own data
  • No stated path to break-even, or a vague one
  • Allocation of the raise across categories left unstated
Defensible projection
  • Growth rate consistent with your own Traction slide data
  • A specific break-even month tied to a named assumption
  • Raise allocation stated clearly across team, marketing, and product
A defensible Seed projection
"Current MRR €2,800, burn €6,200/month. Projected at our actual 18% MoM growth rate, we reach €18K MRR and break-even by month 18, assuming CAC holds within 20% of today's number."

"Our projection used to show a growth curve that had nothing to do with our actual traction numbers. Once we made them the same number, the whole deck became internally consistent, and that consistency was noticed immediately."

What Seed investors check

Current MRR and burn stated precisely. A 12-24 month projection built from your own growth rate, not an industry average. A specific break-even milestone tied to a named assumption. Raise allocation stated across team, marketing, and product.

Key takeaways
  • Anchor the projection to your actual current MRR and burn.
  • Use your own growth rate from the Traction slide, not an industry benchmark.
  • State a specific break-even month, tied to a named assumption.
  • Keep the financial model internally consistent with the rest of the deck.
  • State how the raise is allocated across team, marketing, and product.
