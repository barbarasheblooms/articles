How to choose the right acquisition channel for your business, not the one you find most exciting

SheBloomsGo-to-market

Founders usually pick a channel because a peer used it successfully, not because it fits their price point, sales cycle, or how their buyers actually search for solutions. Here's the framework that should drive the decision instead.

Every acquisition channel has a natural fit with certain business shapes and a poor fit with others. Before testing anything, three questions narrow the field dramatically: what does your customer pay, how long does the decision take, and are you selling to businesses or individuals.

Start with price point

Price point determines how much you can afford to spend acquiring a customer, which in turn determines which channels are even mathematically possible.

Price pointWhat this usually rules inWhat this usually rules out
Low (self-serve, under ~$50/mo or one-time purchase under ~$100)PLG, content/SEO, paid social at scale, referralOutbound sales, event sponsorships — the margin per customer can't cover a rep's time
Mid ($50-500/mo or $1k-10k one-time)Paid search, content, light-touch sales-assist, partnershipsPure PLG rarely covers the whole journey alone; usually paired with a sales-assist layer
High (enterprise, $10k+ contracts)Outbound, partnerships/channel sales, events, account-based approachesPaid social/search alone rarely closes deals this size — they can support awareness but the close needs a relationship

Then layer in sales cycle length

A short sales cycle (impulse or same-week decisions) rewards channels that can convert on first contact — paid, PLG self-serve signup. A long sales cycle (weeks to months of evaluation, multiple stakeholders) rewards channels built for nurturing a relationship over time — content that builds trust before the buyer is ready, outbound that opens the relationship early, partnerships that provide social proof.

Then check B2B vs. B2C buyer behavior

B2CB2B
Typical decision driverIndividual preference, often emotional or impulsiveGroup consensus, ROI justification, risk aversion
Channels that fit naturallyPaid social, influencer, organic search for consumer intent, referralLinkedIn/outbound, content that builds authority, partnerships, events, account-based marketing
What moves the decisionSocial proof, price, immediate gratificationCase studies, peer references, demonstrated ROI, trust built over multiple touches

Put the three together

Overlay price point, sales cycle, and B2B/B2C, and most businesses land clearly in one of two zones:

Two common patterns
  • Low price, short cycle, often B2C or prosumer: the winning channels are usually PLG, content/SEO, and paid social — high volume, low touch, product does the convincing.
  • High price, long cycle, usually B2B: the winning channels are usually outbound, partnerships, and content that builds authority over the evaluation period — low volume, high touch, people do the convincing.

Businesses in the messy middle (mid-price, B2B, medium cycle) typically need a blend: content or paid search to generate interest, followed by a sales-assist motion to close. That blend is exactly why "just do content and see" or "just do sales and see" often disappoints founders in this zone — neither alone matches the buyer's actual path.

A channel that worked for someone else's business is not evidence it will work for yours

The founder whose paid social channel is producing great CAC almost certainly has a lower price point, shorter cycle, or more emotionally-driven buyer than a B2B tool selling a $20k annual contract. Match the channel to your own price point, cycle, and buyer type before testing — it filters out most of the obviously-wrong options before you spend a dollar.

Worked example
A B2B SaaS tool at $200/month, sold to small business owners who decide within a week or two without a committee. This sits in the mid-price, short-cycle, semi-B2B zone: paid search (they're actively searching for a solution to a known problem) and content/SEO (building trust with case studies) are strong fits; heavy outbound is probably premature at this price point, since the margin barely covers a few hours of a rep's time per deal; pure PLG might work if the product is simple enough to self-serve, but likely needs a light sales-assist layer for anyone who has questions before paying.

This framework narrows the field — it doesn't replace testing. Use it to pick 2-3 channels worth testing seriously, not to skip validation entirely.
