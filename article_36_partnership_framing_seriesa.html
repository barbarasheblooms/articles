How to frame the close as a partnership conversation about returns

By SheBlooms 7 min read

At Series A, the close should read as a considered conversation about how the investor gets a return, not a straightforward ask for money.

Risk assessment is more sophisticated at Series A. Investors are thinking specifically about what could break as you scale, and expect you to have already thought about the same things: regulatory exposure, customer concentration, and a credible view on how this eventually returns capital.

Map risk explicitly, rather than let it go unaddressed

Naming regulatory, market, and concentration risks yourself, with a considered response to each, reads as more sophisticated than hoping nobody asks. This is now expected, not optional.

1
Map regulatory and market risks
Name the specific regulatory or market shifts that could affect the business, and your considered view on how exposed you actually are.
2
Address dependency on key customers or channels
If a meaningful share of revenue or growth depends on a small number of customers or a single channel, name it and explain your plan to diversify.
3
State your exit strategy view
M&A or IPO, and a rough sense of timing, shows you are thinking about the full lifecycle of the investment, not just the next 18 months.
Reads as an ask
  • Risks left unaddressed or only raised if the investor asks
  • No mention of customer or channel concentration
  • No stated view on exit strategy
Reads as a partnership
  • Regulatory and market risks named and addressed directly
  • Customer or channel dependency named with a diversification plan
  • A considered exit strategy view, with rough timing
A Series A close framed as a partnership
"Our top 3 customers represent 22% of revenue; we are actively diversifying through the two new distribution partnerships mentioned earlier. Regulatory exposure is limited given our current markets. We see this as an M&A-likely path, with a next round or exit conversation realistic in 24 to 36 months."
What Series A investors check

Regulatory or market risks mapped explicitly. Dependency on key customers or channels addressed with a diversification plan. Exit strategy stated with a rough timing view. The overall framing as a partnership conversation about returns, not just an ask.

Key takeaways
  • Map regulatory and market risks yourself rather than waiting to be asked.
  • Address dependency on key customers or channels with a diversification plan.
  • State a considered view on exit strategy and rough timing.
  • Frame the close as a partnership conversation about returns, not just an ask.
  • Investors expect sophisticated risk assessment to already be part of your own thinking.
